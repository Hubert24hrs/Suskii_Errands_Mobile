## How the pass worked

Earlier audits read one side against its own design. This one read the **seam**: what the apps
actually send to the backend, and what the backend can actually answer.

1. **Branches.** 21 remote branches besides `main`; seven carry commits that are not in `main` by
   patch id. Every file version on them was compared with `main`'s history and every path with
   `main`'s tree. Nothing is missing (Z.6).
2. **Calls against the contract, mechanically.** A checker that reads every RPC, argument, table,
   column and grant the Dart and TypeScript data layers use, and compares them with the generated
   contract. It was proved by planting six kinds of mistake before trusting its first clean result.
   54 of the 138 contract functions are called by the apps; **0 mismatches**. It now runs in CI
   (`contracts/tools/check_frontend_calls.py`, `contracts.yaml`), so the next drift fails a build.
3. **Where the apps stop.** Every place a real-backend repository throws `featureUnavailable`,
   reports zeros, or has no Supabase implementation at all, traced to its cause.
4. **Every suite**, then the end-to-end suite against a real local Supabase.

The method was the one that found Y.28: **run it**. The admin console was wired behind the same
interfaces its mock implements, so every screen kept its shape, and then driven through a browser
against a real local Supabase as six staff accounts, with every flow checking the database
afterwards rather than the screen. Most of the findings below were found that way, not by reading.

### What the console does on the real backend

Every screen reads and writes through functions the backend already had or that part 2 added:
sign-in with password and TOTP (enrolment on first use, `aal2` required, customers refused), the
dashboard (`admin_overview`, money columns only for super admin and finance), the directory
(search only from four characters, suspensions with an end date), KYC review with the document
opened through a 60-second signed URL issued after the access row is written, business
verification, disputes resolved at the amounts `dispute_resolution_quote` shows, support tickets,
SOS (live on `ops:sos`), risk flags, payments with two-person withdrawals, configuration and staff
and promos as four-eyes proposals, analytics, and the audit log. Three things say "not available"
on purpose: the AI assistant (GCP billing), the DAU chart (no analytics view counts distinct people, and per-user
analytics needs the keyed pseudonym ADR-0007 puts in KMS) and reviewing a referral flag (blocked referrals are decided, not queued).
