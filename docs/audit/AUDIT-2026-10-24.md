## How the pass worked

1. **Parity, screen by screen.** Every route of the app's customer and provider sides was listed
   against the website. The website had the customer side; it had **no provider side at all**, and
   lacked SOS, the display name, the theme choice, the SMS and marketing switches and a mode switch.
   All of it is built (below), on the same repositories and RPCs the app uses.
2. **Run it, don't read it.** The mock suites were green throughout and found none of what follows.
   A new suite drives the website against a real local Supabase (`tests/web-customer-e2e`), each step
   checking the database afterwards. Every High finding below came out of its first runs.
3. **Make the mocks honest.** Two findings existed only because the mocks were kinder than the server
   (a 4-digit PIN; idempotency keys scoped per operation). Both mocks now behave like the server.

## Parity after this pass

**Checked mechanically, not by eye.** `contracts/tools/check_screen_parity.py` reads every
repository method the app's screens call (through the Riverpod providers) and every one the
website's screens call (through the repository singletons), folds the names where a method lives
in a differently named repository, and fails on anything left on one side that is not a listed
platform difference. Its first run found AA.10's two gaps; after the fixes it reports **116 methods
on the app's screens, 118 on the website's, 0 gaps**. What remains on one side only is the native
Google/Apple sheet against the browser redirect, and three reads one app streams and the other
reads on load. Proved by planting a removed feature (it fails) before trusting the clean result;
runs in `contracts.yaml` whenever either app changes.
